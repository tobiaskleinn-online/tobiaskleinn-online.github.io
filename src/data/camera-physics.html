Simulation & calculation details

The scene is a stylized thin-lens illustration, not a photograph or a lens-specific bokeh simulator. Use Top view to change foreground, subject, background and focus distances. Portrait, landscape and macro use different objects and distance ranges. Exposure is held constant to isolate framing and blur. Perspective stays fixed because the camera does not move. Sensor aspect ratio changes the preview crop.

Depth of field uses a circle of confusion equal to sensor diagonal / 1500, intended for a conventional viewing-size comparison. Actual perceived sharpness depends on enlargement, viewing distance, eyesight, diffraction and lens performance. Hyperfocal H = f²/(N·c) + f. Distances in the simulation are measured from the equivalent thin-lens plane. Real camera focus marks are measured from the sensor plane. Macro results are idealized: pupil magnification, focus breathing, effective f-number, diffraction and lens-specific minimum focus distances are not modeled. Horizontal angle of view uses 2·atan(sensor width / 2f).

01

Focal length

Focal length is the optical distance from the lens’s rear principal plane to the sensor when focused at infinity.

More details & references

Focal length is the distance from the rear principal plane of a lens to its rear focal point, measured in millimeters. When focused at infinity, the sensor is at this focal plane.

For a simple thin lens, the principal plane passes through its center. In a real lens with several elements, it is an optical reference plane and may lie outside the glass. Focal length is not the length of the lens barrel.

Parallel rays from a very distant point meet at the focal point. The diagram shows this infinity-focus condition, independently of the focus-distance slider.

02

Focal length changes the view

Shorter focal length: wider view. Longer focal length: narrower view and a larger image of the subject.

More details & human vision
Human vision reference

50 mm on full frame is a common “normal lens” reference for natural-looking images. This is a photographic convention, not the eye’s actual focal length or its full visual field.

The eye’s optical focal length

A relaxed eye has roughly 60 diopters of optical power, equivalent to about 16.7 mm focal length in air. This is not a camera-format equivalence: the retina is curved, sharpness varies across it, and vision combines both eyes and eye movements.

A shorter focal length captures a wider angle. A longer focal length captures a narrower angle and makes the same subject larger in the frame.

Rectilinear lenses only. Fisheye lenses use a different projection. Camera position stays fixed; changing focal length alone does not change perspective.

03

In focus and out of focus

In focus, rays from a point meet on the sensor. Out of focus, they form a blur disc. A smaller aperture reduces that disc.

Sharpness limit

Is there a maximum blur size?

There is no single maximum for all lenses and scenes. The physical blur diameter depends on focal length, aperture, focus setting and object distance. The circle of confusion is a chosen sharpness tolerance, not a physical cap on blur. Larger discs are still possible; they are simply outside the accepted depth of field.

This tool uses sensor diagonal ÷ 1500 as the acceptable diameter. It does not represent a universal pixel-size or eye-resolution limit. Output size, viewing distance and required detail change what looks sharp.

The drawing rescales both pink spots together to fit. Its largest drawn spot is not a maximum physical blur size; read the diameter in millimeters.

More details & bokeh

Are these bokeh balls? The pink discs represent a defocused point’s image on the sensor. Small bright lights can appear as bokeh discs in a photo. They are not physical spheres. “Bokeh” describes the appearance of the out-of-focus image, including softness, shape and texture. Blade shape and lens aberrations affect it.

Nikon · bokeh ↗

In focus: rays from an object point converge at the sensor plane. Out of focus: they converge in front of or behind the sensor, so the sensor records a disc instead of a point. Its diameter is the geometric blur circle.

A smaller aperture narrows the ray cone. The same focusing error then produces a smaller blur circle. Compare the current aperture with an aperture two stops smaller below.

Depth of field

The range of object distances that meet a chosen sharpness threshold without refocusing. In this tool, the geometric blur circle must be no larger than the selected sensor’s circle-of-confusion limit.

Depth of focus

The permitted range of sensor positions around the image plane for a fixed subject and lens setting. This is on the image side of the lens; it is different from depth of field.

EQUIVALENT SETTINGS

What stays the same?

The equivalent focal length matches diagonal angle of view. Matching depth of field also needs the equivalent f-number, the same camera position and focus distance, and comparable output size. Different aspect ratios do not match both frame edges.

The equivalent f-number describes depth of field, not exposure. f/2 stays f/2 for exposure on any format. Using the displayed equivalent aperture would require adjusting shutter speed or ISO to retain brightness.

How the estimate works

Earth’s rotation moves stars across the sensor. This local, small-angle model converts that motion into pixels using focal length, estimated pixel pitch and declination. Square pixels and the full active sensor area are assumed.

p = 1000 × √(A / M)
t = P × (p / 1000) / (f × ω × cos δ)
ω = 2π / 86164.0905 rad/s

A: sensor area (mm²), M: pixel count, P: allowed pixel movement, p: pixel pitch (µm), f: focal length (mm), δ: declination.

The estimate applies near the image center. Very wide-angle corners may trail more. Aperture changes star size and brightness but not the geometric movement used here. Focus, lens aberrations, noise and viewing size also affect the result.

The 500 rule uses 500 ÷ (focal length × diagonal crop factor). It ignores pixel resolution and can allow visible trails. The pixel limit is a personal tolerance, not a guarantee of pinpoint stars.
